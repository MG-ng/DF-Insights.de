How likely is a day on which wind, solar and the other renewables cover at most 35% of Germany's electricity demand? The forecast runs every member of three global weather ensembles (ECMWF IFS, ECMWF's machine-learning ensemble AIFS and NOAA GEFS, over 130 weather scenarios) through an energy model trained on ten years of SMARD generation data. Which weather regime is coming?

Most Dunkelflauten happen under a few large-scale weather patterns, above all blocking highs over Central Europe or Scandinavia, which calm the wind for days (Mockert et al. 2023, Grams et al. 2017). Each ECMWF member's sea level pressure forecast is sorted into one of seven regimes, learned from ERA5 data with k-means clustering. The legend gives how often each regime brought a Dunkelflaute day in winter.

How good is this forecast? Backtest on data the model never saw

The energy model was fitted on 2015 to 2022. Below, it is scored on days after that with forecasts as they were issued at the time (archived ECMWF runs from Open-Meteo), with the error width calibrated on 2024 and the scores computed on 2025 and 2026 only. A Brier skill score of 1 is a perfect forecast, 0 is no better than climatology (the usual rate for the time of year with today's fleet of wind and solar plants), below 0 is worse.

Hit rate: share of Dunkelflaute days that got a probability of 50% or more. False alarms: share of those warnings that did not come true. Persistence assumes the situation of the last observed day continues.

How it works

  1. Where the plants are. 16 weather points cover the offshore clusters in the North and Baltic Sea, the windy north and east and the solar-heavy south.
  2. Physics first. Hourly 100 m wind speed goes through a turbine power curve (cut-in 3 m/s, full power from 11.5 m/s, storm shutdown at 25 m/s) before it is averaged to days. Averaging the wind first would hide that output rises with the cube of the wind speed.
  3. Learned from SMARD. Ridge regressions map these capacity factors, solar radiation and temperature to SMARD's onshore, offshore and solar generation, and calendar (weekdays, holidays, Christmas) plus temperature to demand. A capacity index (actual divided by modelled generation over the last 365 days, extrapolated) follows the growing fleet without needing a plant register.
  4. Many weathers, one answer. Every ensemble member gives a renewable share per day. Each gets the energy model's own error (measured on held-out years), and the average over all members is the probability. After day 7 the probability is gradually blended towards climatology (up to 35% weight), because skill fades there: in the backtest a single forecast run had no skill left at 6 to 7 days.
  5. Runs of calm days. Because each ensemble member is one consistent weather scenario, the chance of several Dunkelflaute days in a row is counted member by member (with the model error added as day-to-day correlated noise) instead of being guessed from single-day probabilities.
  6. Honest scores. Every number above comes from data that was not used for fitting.

Data: SMARD (Bundesnetzagentur), ERA5 reanalysis and archived/ensemble forecasts via Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0). Code: work/prediction/. Retrain with python -m prediction.fetch_training_data and python -m prediction.train.
